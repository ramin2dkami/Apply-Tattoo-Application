# 001 — Body region picker

**Status:** shipped
**Roadmap item:** Now / Prototype v1

## What it does

"Where do you want it?" The customer sees one neutral outline figure and taps the parts
of the body they want — one region, or several if the piece spans a joint — then zooms
in with the placement canvas ready.

## Why it matters

It's the first screen, so it sets whether this feels like a tool or a form. Pointing at
a body is also a lower-vocabulary interaction than picking from a list of names — the
customer who doesn't know "ribs" from "ribcage" can still point. And it supplies the
geometry everything else depends on: sizing and contouring both read from the region's
metadata.

## How it works

**One figure, drawn as neutral line art.** Outlines only — no fill colour, no skin
tone. See `context/decisions.md` D5 for why. It's generated by
`assets/tools/build_figure.py` from an anatomical profile in centimetres on a 180 cm
body at a fixed 10 px/cm, mirrored about the centreline.

**Regions are viewBox windows into that one drawing.** A region is a `y` range plus
which surface it belongs to (arm, leg, torso). From that the build derives the zoom
window, the reference dimension, and a sampled silhouette. Because the art is vector,
zooming to a region is free and needs no second asset.

**Parts are what the customer picks**, matching the mockup: head, neck & torso, back,
hips, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg. Each part is emitted as its own asset
*and* assembled into the whole figure used as the picker — both generated from the same
profile, so they cannot drift (`context/decisions.md` D8).

Each part carries a rotation model: arms and legs rotate continuously through 360°,
head/torso/hips step between front and back views. See `specs/007-rotate-part.md`.

Regions (forearm, upper arm, thigh, calf, chest, stomach) survive underneath as
sub-areas of a part. They carry the reference dimensions that make sizing exact, and
they are what a spanning selection merges.

**Multi-select and chains.** Regions sharing a continuous surface form a *chain* —
`arm-r` is `upper-arm-r + forearm-r`, `leg-r` is `thigh-r + calf-r`, `torso` is
`chest + abdomen`. Selecting several regions in a chain merges their silhouettes into
one surface and widens the zoom window to their union, so a sleeve crossing the elbow
is a single placement in a single coordinate space (see `context/decisions.md` D6).
Selecting across chains is allowed — the customer sees both — but each surface is
contoured on its own; artwork genuinely crossing from torso to arm is a Later item and
needs a junction model we do not have.

`regions.json` shape:

```json
{
  "figure": { "art": "front.svg", "heightCm": 180, "pxPerCm": 10 },
  "regions": [{
    "id": "forearm-r",
    "label": "Forearm",
    "viewBox": [0, 0, 0, 0],
    "referenceDimension": { "label": "elbow to wrist", "cm": 26, "px": 260 },
    "surface": { "model": "cylinder", "wrapDegrees": 150, "silhouette": [] }
  }]
}
```

The figure being 180 cm at 10 px/cm is what makes `004` (real-world sizing) true by
construction; `surface.silhouette` is what makes `005` (contour warp) possible. The
build asserts every region's span against its stated reference and fails loudly on a
mismatch.

Picker interaction: tap a region directly on the figure. Regions need visible hit
areas — a tappable outline that appears on hover/press — since an undifferentiated
outline drawing gives no affordance on its own.

## Explicitly not in this

Back view, body types, and adjustable proportions. All parked in Next — but the profile
is a table of numbers, so adding them stays a data change rather than a code change.
Skin tones are gone from the roadmap entirely: the figure is neutral by design.

## Open questions

- ~~Where do the illustrations come from?~~ Resolved: generated from a profile table.
- ~~Which skin tones?~~ Resolved: none. The figure is neutral outline.
- **The drawing is serviceable, not beautiful.** It reads as a human figure and the
  geometry is correct, but it is a weaker drawing than the reference it's based on.
  Worth deciding whether to invest in the linework or vectorise a reference — see
  `REVIEW.md` 2026-08-27.
- Regions give no affordance on an outline drawing. Needs a visible hit state, and
  the design of that isn't settled.
- One figure means one set of proportions. Does a customer with a very different body
  trust the size readout? This is an artist question, not a drawing question.
- Multi-select needs an interaction that reads on a phone. Tap-to-toggle is the
  obvious answer; whether people discover that spanning is possible is not obvious.
- ~~The back view~~ Resolved: drawn. Reused the existing silhouette (body width is the
  same front-to-back) and added a `BACK_DETAILS` table — spine, shoulder blades, lats,
  lower-back crease, glutes, hamstrings. First pass drew details as three broad arcs
  that visually merged into one dome across the lower back; fixed by tightening each
  curve to hug its own anatomical region rather than sweeping across the full width.
- Does picking a whole arm and then rotating beat picking "inner forearm" from a list?
  Probably, but it's more taps.
