Rotating a limb costs no extra drawings

A limb is a stack of circular cross-sections, so its outline looks the same from every angle — turning an arm changes which skin faces you, not the silhouette. That makes 360° rotation one extra term in the maths rather than a set of new illustrations. Below: one drawing, one tattoo fixed to the same patch of skin, five rotations. The anatomy lines are on the surface too, so front detail slides away as back detail slides in.

Which parts rotate freely, and which don't

Arms and legs are near enough to cylinders to rotate continuously from one drawing. Head, torso and hips are not — their cross-section is nowhere near circular and the front and back are genuinely different pictures, which is exactly why your mockup has both. Those get discrete views instead of free rotation.
